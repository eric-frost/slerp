# What each reader covers

This page says, kind by kind, what Slerp reads in each format and what it leaves out.  The plain list of every format with its file extensions, generated from Slerp's format table, is in [formats.md](formats.md).

## By kind

| Kind | Formats |
|---|---|
| GPU textures | KTX2 (every vkFormat; zstd, zlib and Basis Universal: ETC1S, UASTC LDR/HDR, XUASTC, XUBC7), KTX1, DDS (legacy and DX10, BC1-7, BC6H, and the video formats YUY2, UYVY, NV12, P010 and their kin), raw ASTC; 3D ASTC volume textures, shown a slice at a time |
| Images | Radiance HDR (tone mapped), 16-bit greyscale PNG heightmaps (stretched to their range) |
| Models | glTF 2.0 and GLB (including meshopt, Draco, quantized and Basis-textured files), plain Draco `.drc` meshes and point clouds, VRM 0.x and 1.0, FBX, OBJ, STL, PLY, Collada, USD/USDA/USDC/USDZ (with UsdSkel skeletons, skins, blend shapes and animation clips, and point instancers), MagicaVoxel, Blockbench `.bbmodel` (cubes and meshes, embedded textures, animations, Blockbench 5 armatures) |
| Materials | MaterialX, USD materials, Godot `.tres`; shown on a ball, or as the preview picture the material ships |
| Gaussian splats | 3DGS PLY (with spherical harmonics), SuperSplat compressed PLY, `.splat`, SPZ, PlayCanvas SOG |
| Characters | MetaHuman `.dna` files (head and body, with live RigLogic face controls in the viewer) |
| Blender | `.blend` files from 2.4 to 5.x: thumbnails show the preview Blender stores in the file (as KDE's own Blender thumbnailer does), and the viewer opens the scene with its collections, instances, materials and image textures, and the common modifiers applied (Mirror, Array, Subdivision Surface, Solidify, Triangulate, Weld, Edge Split, Smooth by Angle) |
| Games | Quake and Half-Life BSP maps (with lightmaps), Quake/Half-Life MDL, Source engine MDL (versions 44 to 49: Half-Life 2 to CS:GO and Garry's Mod, with the `.vvd` and `.vtx` beside it; VMT/VTF materials from the game's `materials/` folders or VPK packs, normal maps, skin families as a picker, body groups as switchable parts, flexes as morph sliders, sequences as clips), MD2, MD3, WAD2/WAD3 texture packs, Doom WADs and GZDoom's PK3/PK7 archives (title screen and automaps as thumbnails, every map as a 3D level in the viewer), Quake and Quake 2 PAK, Godot PCK, Valve VPK and Build GRP archives (a contact sheet of the pictures inside), Unreal `.uasset` thumbnails; level editors' map sources: Quake, Half-Life, Quake 2 and 3 `.map` (TrenchBroom, J.A.C.K., Radiant) with textures from their WADs, and Source `.vmf` (Hammer) with displacements and VTF materials, loose or in VPK packs |
| Rigged characters | MikuMikuDance PMX 2.0/2.1 and PMD models (skins, morphs, materials with their textures; SDEF and QDEF skinning blended linearly; toon ramps, sphere maps and outlines left out) with VMD motions beside the model or added in the viewer (eased keys baked per frame, face morphs; IK and physics not run), Doom 3 `.md5mesh` with the `.md5anim` files beside it, Inter-Quake `.iqm` and `.iqe` with their animations, Source/GoldSrc `.smd` reference meshes with the animation SMDs in their folder, Unreal ActorX `.psk`/`.pskx` (extra UVs, normals and morph targets; vertex colours not drawn) with the `.psa` sets beside them; animation files on their own open as a skeleton for "Add Animations" |
| Minecraft | Schematics: Sponge `.schem` (WorldEdit, versions 1 to 3), Litematica `.litematic` (every region in place), structure-block `.nbt` files and MCEdit/Schematica `.schematic` (numeric ids from before 1.13): blocks greedy-meshed with hidden faces left out; slabs, stairs, fences, walls, panes, plants, carpets, doors, trapdoors, torches and other common shapes approximated; colours from Slerp's own table with small patterns, or the block textures of a Minecraft jar or resource pack found on the computer (never copied); builds of tens of millions of blocks thinned for thumbnails; a Litematica file's saved preview picture as its thumbnail; block counts by kind in the inspector |
| Roblox | Places and models, binary `.rbxl`/`.rbxm` and XML `.rbxlx`/`.rbxmx` (and XML `.rbxl` from before 2013), read offline by Slerp's own bounded readers: the Workspace's parts (blocks, balls, cylinders, wedges, corner wedges, trusses, spawn locations, seats), special, block and cylinder meshes, colours and BrickColors, transparency, reflectance and materials as colour and roughness (Roblox's material textures are neither shipped nor downloaded), models and folders as nodes, invisible and negative parts in a hidden node, unions from the triangles the file keeps or their source parts, and smooth terrain with water.  Mesh parts and unions whose geometry lives on Roblox's servers are drawn by the collision hulls the file keeps, else as boxes, with a note counting them; decals and textures name server images and are not drawn.  Places open as levels at their spawn location; thumbnails leave out far-away outliers and cut a baseplate down to the build on it; the inspector counts instances by class and how each union and mesh part was drawn |
| Maya and 3ds Max | Maya ASCII `.ma` and binary `.mb` (claimed by content, since Mathematica and others use the extensions) and 3ds Max `.max`, read without the vendors' software.  Maya: the DAG with pivots, rotate orders and joint orientation; meshes stored in the file (faces with holes, UV sets, hard edges, stored normals, colour sets shown when Display Colors is on); meshes whose history is a primitive (`polyCube`, `polySphere`, `polyCylinder`, `polyCone`, `polyPlane`, `polyTorus`) rebuilt from its parameters; meshes behind deformers in their stored shape, skinned meshes posed as their joints and animation curves stand at the scene's current time (the bind pose where constraints drive the joints); lambert, blinn, phong, standardSurface, aiStandardSurface, openPBRSurface and StingrayPBS materials with file textures found beside the file or in the project's `sourceimages`.  3ds Max: node hierarchy and transforms, Editable Mesh and Editable Poly objects (UVs, smoothing groups, stored normals, material ids), the standard primitives rebuilt from their parameters, TurboSmooth and MeshSmooth applied, Standard, Physical and Multi/Sub-Object materials with bitmap textures, and the wire colour for objects without a material.  Meshes that exist only as construction history, other modifiers, and references to other files are listed in the inspector.  Thumbnails of `.max` files stay the picture 3ds Max saved in them; Maya files carry none, so theirs are rendered |
| 2D games | Aseprite sprites (every colour mode, blend mode, group and tilemap layer; tags play in the viewer), Tiled maps (TMX and JSON; orthogonal, isometric, staggered and hexagonal; external and image-collection tilesets, object layers), LDtk projects and level files (IntGrid, tile, auto-tile and entity layers; the whole world as the thumbnail, each level as a part in the viewer), Piskel `.piskel` sprites (layers with opacity, frames playing at the file's rate) and Pyxel Edit `.pyxel` documents (layers with their blend modes and opacity) |
| Game engines | Godot scenes (`.tscn`, `.escn`, binary `.scn`) and mesh resources, Unity scenes and prefabs saved as text (`.unity`, `.prefab`): the models, meshes, materials and nested scenes they refer to are found in the project around them; large scenes open as levels |
| 3D applications | The preview pictures stored in SketchUp `.skp` and 3ds Max `.max` files (their thumbnails; the viewer opens the model itself, see above), Cinema 4D `.c4d` (R13 on) and ZBrush `.ztl`/`.zpr`/`.zbr` files, whose geometry is closed |
| Creative apps | The preview pictures stored in Procreate, Affinity (`.afphoto`, `.afdesign`, `.afpub`, `.af`), Sketch, Figma local copies (`.fig`, told apart from Xfig drawings), Clip Studio Paint `.clip`, Paint.NET `.pdn`, InDesign `.indd`, Adobe XD and FreeCAD `.FCStd` files |
| Retro computer art | Atari ST DEGAS (`.pi1`-`.pi3`, compressed `.pc1`-`.pc3`), NEOchrome `.neo` and Tiny Stuff `.tny`; Commodore 64 Koala, Art Studio, Doodle and hires bitmaps; ZX Spectrum `.scr` (standard, Timex hi-colour and hi-res) and Amstrad CPC `.scr` (mode from a `.pal` beside it, else guessed); MSX screens 2, 5, 6, 7, 8 and 12 (with the `.pl5` palette beside them); Apple II hi-res and double hi-res; Atari 8-bit `.mic` and `.g15`; BBC Micro `.bbc`; Amiga IFF ILBM and PBM (HAM, Extra Half-Brite, masks, true colour): every pixel at the shape it had on the original screen, with hard edges.  Dumps with no header are told apart by extension and exact size |
| ANSI art | BBC and demoscene `.ans` files (and any file ending in a SAUCE record) drawn on a character grid with a built-in 8x16 CP437 font: colours (16, 256 and 24-bit), cursor movement, iCE colours, width and the title, author, group and date from SAUCE |
| Fantasy consoles | PICO-8 `.p8` text cartridges (the label, else the sprite sheet) and TIC-80 `.tic` cartridges (the cover, else the sprite sheet in the cartridge's palette) |
| Game images | XNA/MonoGame `.xnb` textures and sprite fonts (LZX and LZ4 compressed or not, DXT and packed formats), Blizzard `.blp` (Warcraft III BLP1 and WoW BLP2), PowerVR `.pvr` (v1 to v3, PVRTC, ETC, DXT and plain pixels) and `.pkm`, Build engine `.art` tile sets (Duke Nukem 3D, Shadow Warrior, Blood: a gallery drawn with the `PALETTE.DAT` beside it), Quake and Half-Life `.spr` and Quake 2 `.sp2` sprites, and sprite-sheet descriptors (libGDX and Spine `.atlas`, TexturePacker `.tpsheet`, TexturePacker and Aseprite JSON) shown as the sheet with every sprite outlined and listed |
| Metafiles | Windows Metafile `.wmf` (with or without the placeable header), Enhanced Metafile `.emf` (GDI records and the EMF+ records of Office and .NET: gradients, transparency, curves, images, strings), their gzip forms `.wmz` and `.emz`, and Macintosh PICT `.pct`/`.pict` (versions 1 and 2, vector shapes, patterns, text, packed bitmaps): replayed on the CPU onto a white page at the file's own proportions, with text in a system font when one is found (a built-in line font otherwise) |
| CorelDRAW and Visio | The preview pictures stored in CorelDRAW `.cdr`/`.cdt` (versions 7 to 13 and X4 to X7, and later ones that keep the X7 layout) and Corel Presentation Exchange `.cmx`, and in Visio `.vsd`, `.vsdx` and the other Visio file types (small metafiles, drawn by the metafile code) |
| CAD previews | The pictures CAD programs save in their files, whose geometry is closed: AutoCAD `.dwg` (R13 to the current version; a BMP, PNG or metafile), SolidWorks `.sldprt`, `.sldasm`, `.slddrw` (the OLE files of 2014 and older, and the record container of 2015 on), Autodesk Inventor `.ipt`, `.iam`, `.idw`, `.ipn`, Revit `.rvt`, `.rfa`, `.rte`, Solid Edge `.par`, `.psm`, `.asm`, `.dft` (told apart from assembler source and parity files by content), Autodesk DWF and DWFx (every sheet's picture, the sheets as parts in the viewer) and Fusion `.f3d` (deflate and Zstandard members) |
| App previews | The pictures saved in Apple Keynote, Pages and Numbers documents (iWork 2013 on and iWork '09), Paint Shop Pro images, frames and tubes (`.pspimage`, `.psp`, `.pspframe`, `.psptube`, `.tub`; Paint Shop Pro 5 to PaintShop Pro 2023: the stored thumbnail, the merged picture, or a single-layer document's layer), Pixelmator Pro `.pxd` (its QuickLook picture) and PaintTool SAI (`.sai`'s encrypted thumbnail, `.sai2`'s thumbnail and full-size merged canvas).  None of these is offered in "Open with", so Slerp never becomes their default app; `slerp view` still opens them |
| Documents | PDF (every version, object streams, damaged cross-reference tables repaired, RC4 and AES encryption when no password is needed to open), Adobe Illustrator `.ai` (files saved with PDF compatibility, the default since Illustrator 9, show their artboards; older PostScript ones go the EPS way), EPS (`.eps`, `.epsi`, `.epsf`) and PostScript `.ps`.  PDF pages are drawn by `hayro`, a renderer written in Rust, in a child process with a time and memory limit, since PDFs from the internet are hostile; the viewer shows one page or artboard at a time and draws it again as it zooms, so text stays sharp, with the pages as a picker and Page Up, Page Down and the arrows stepping through them, and the title, author, producer and page size in the inspector.  Files that need a password to open are refused with a message.  Thumbnails show the picture the file stores when it has one (a page's `/Thumb`, the thumbnail Illustrator writes into the XMP metadata), else the first page.  EPS files show the preview they store (TIFF or Windows Metafile behind a DOS EPS header, an EPSI bitmap, or an XMP thumbnail); without one, and for PostScript, the system's Ghostscript draws them when it is installed (run as a separate program in safe mode with time and memory limits; Ghostscript is never bundled), and the viewer offers both the stored preview and Ghostscript's drawing.  Thumbnails of PDF, EPS and PostScript stay off by default, since KDE's and GNOME's own thumbnailers make them; Illustrator thumbnails are on |
| Colour tables | 3D and 1D lookup tables (`.cube`, `.3dl`), shown on a photo split before and after; the viewer grades any photo dropped on it |
| Colour palettes | Adobe Swatch Exchange `.ase`, Photoshop `.aco`, Adobe colour tables `.act`, GIMP and Inkscape `.gpl`, Krita `.kpl`, JASC and RIFF `.pal`, Lospec `.hex` and Procreate `.swatches`: a grid of swatches as the thumbnail, and in the viewer each colour with its name, hex value and CMYK, Lab or HSB numbers, copied by a click |
| 3D printing and CNC | G-code (`.gcode`, `.gco`; `.nc`, `.ngc`, `.tap` and others by content) and Prusa's binary `.bgcode` (deflate, heatshrink, MeatPack): the thumbnail is the largest picture the slicer embedded (PrusaSlicer, OrcaSlicer, Bambu Studio, Cura's thumbnail script, Creality, Snapmaker Luban, MKS, Weedo; PNG, JPEG, QOI), else the toolpath drawn as extrusions coloured by feature; the viewer shows the toolpath with a layer slider, feature and travel toggles, and the slicer's print time and filament.  Mill and laser programs draw their cuts |
| GPS tracks | GPX, TCX, FIT (Garmin, Wahoo, Hammerhead and other bike computers and watches), KML, KMZ and GeoJSON: the route from above coloured by elevation or speed as the thumbnail; in the viewer the route in 3D over its own exaggerated elevation with a curtain to the ground, an elevation profile linked to it, colours by elevation, speed, heart rate, cadence or power, and distance, climb, moving time, speeds and sensor averages.  No map tiles, nothing from the network |
| Medical images | DICOM (`.dcm`, and the extension-less files CDs hold, found by their `DICM` marker or by how a data set starts): CT, MR, X-ray, ultrasound and colour images, explicit and implicit VR in either byte order, deflated, with uncompressed, RLE, JPEG (baseline, 12-bit and lossless), JPEG-LS and JPEG 2000 pixels.  Thumbnails show the middle frame in the stored brightness window, with true proportions; the viewer drags the window with the right mouse button (CT presets for brain, lung, bone, soft tissue and abdomen), plays cine loops, steps through a series in slice order and lists every tag, keeping names, IDs and other patient details hidden until you ask.  DICOMDIR files open their first image and list the studies and series |
| Volumes and arrays | NIfTI-1 and NIfTI-2 (`.nii`, `.nii.gz`, and Analyze `.hdr`/`.img` pairs, either byte order, with scaling), MetaImage (`.mha`, `.mhd` with `.raw` or `.zraw`) and NRRD (`.nrrd`, `.nhdr`; raw, gzip, bzip2 and text): brain scans and other 3D and 4D medical volumes, NumPy `.npy` and `.npz` (every number type and byte order, C and Fortran order; pictures, colour images, stacks and plain arrays; pickled objects are refused, never loaded), NetCDF (classic, 64-bit and CDF-5, and NetCDF-4) and HDF5 (including MATLAB v7.3 `.mat`).  Thumbnails show the middle slice of the first time point in the 1 to 99 percent brightness window, grey for scans and Viridis for measurements.  Volumes stay in the file and are cut one plane at a time, so a half-gigabyte scan opens in milliseconds; the viewer switches between axial, coronal and sagittal planes (radiological layout, with a switch for neurological), steps through slices with the wheel, drags the window with the right mouse button, offers colour maps, a 4D volume slider, a maximum intensity projection and the value under the pointer in voxel and millimetre coordinates, and lists the header.  Gzipped volumes are inflated only as far as the slice shown (`volumeMemoryMb` sets how much the viewer may hold in memory) |
| Microscopy | TIFF stacks, 16-bit, 32 and 64-bit float, tiled, BigTIFF and OME-TIFF (all of OME's dimension orders, channels with their colours, companion files) and ImageJ hyperstacks, and whole-slide scans (Aperio `.svs`, Hamamatsu `.ndpi`, Leica `.scn`, Philips and generic pyramids): a slide's thumbnail comes from its smallest level, never the full picture, and the other levels, label and macro images are parts you can open. | Astronomy | FITS (`.fits`, `.fit`, `.fts`, `.fits.fz`) and PixInsight XISF: the first image in the file (many files keep an empty primary unit and the picture in an extension; the others are listed as parts), 8 to 64-bit integers and 32 and 64-bit floats with `BZERO`/`BSCALE`/`BLANK`, three planes as colour, other plane counts as layers, one-shot-colour mosaics (`BAYERPAT`) demosaiced, tile-compressed images (Rice, gzip, PLIO and HCOMPRESS, with the dithered quantisation of floating-point ones), XISF in either byte order with zlib, lz4, lz4hc and zstd (with or without byte shuffling) blocks. | Netpbm | PAM (`.pam`: 8 and 16-bit, grey, RGB and CMYK with alpha) and farbfeld (`.ff`) |

## Doom and GZDoom maps

A Doom WAD opens on its first map as a 3D level, in first person at the player's start, with a map picker and the 2D automaps a click away.  Doom, Hexen and UDMF maps are read; a PWAD's maps borrow textures and flats from an IWAD (`doom2.wad`, `freedoom2.wad` and the like) found beside it, in a folder next to its folder, in `DOOMWADDIR`/`DOOMWADPATH` or in `/usr/share/games/doom`.  A map unit is an inch across and 1.2 inches tall, because Doom showed its 320x200 frames on 4:3 screens, which puts the 56-unit player at 1.7 m.  `slerp info <wad> --part MAP07` describes one map, and a view state's `"part"` picks the map `slerp render` draws.

Maps made for GZDoom render with what GZDoom adds: sloped floors and ceilings (UDMF plane equations, vertex heights, `Plane_Align`, `Plane_Copy` and the slope things), 3D floors (solid, liquid and translucent slabs, and the light they cast below), coloured light and fog with GZDoom's light modes, glowing planes, panned, scaled and rotated flats, per-wall texture scale and light, translucent lines, and GLDEFS sky boxes.  PK3 and PK7 archives (with TEXTURES, ANIMDEFS, MAPINFO and GLDEFS) are read piece by piece, so on one laptop each map of a 600 MB megawad opened in under a second.  Scripts (ACS, ZScript), dynamic lights (shown as markers), portals (left open) and models are left out: a map shows its start state.

## Godot and Unity projects

A Godot or Unity scene is read together with its project: Godot's `res://` paths from the folder holding `project.godot`, Unity's GUIDs through every `.meta` file under `Assets/` (an index cached in `~/.cache/slerp/unity/`, refreshed when a scene names a GUID it lacks).  Placed models are loaded once however often they appear.  Unity scenes saved in binary form are refused (Project Settings, Editor, Asset Serialization: Force Text); Unity terrain and CSG subtraction aren't drawn.
